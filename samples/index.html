Decisions you can see.

01 / Marketing site

Step 03 · Make it visible October 2026 Claude Design

Seven homepages on one canvas, and the one that shipped

What it is

A rebuild of the Foresera homepage with its solution pages, a header exploration, and a 16-slide walkthrough built from the same source, so the deck and the site cannot drift apart.

Seven homepage versions sit side by side on the canvas, so the comparison is between whole pages, not sentences.

The decision it served

What Foresera claims in public, in what order, and with what proof. The page settled on three verbs, Find it all, Fix it once, Prove it always, and every section below the fold has to earn its place under one of them.

Version 1 opened on a scan-your-domain box. By version 7 the page opens on the three verbs and a walkthrough booking, with the self-serve path moved down into a comparison of the two ways to work.

Why it looks the way it does

  • Every figure is sourced. The design file carries a screen map back to the product repository and the public file the crawl numbers came from, and the copy was aligned to what the repository can prove. The Title II deadlines were corrected to the extended dates in the same pass.
  • The "Why now" section quotes a survey of state accessibility officers and cites it on the page, so a buyer can check the premise instead of taking it on trust.
  • "Fix it once" is interactive: four files, fifteen copies, pay for four. The pricing argument is something a visitor does, not something they read.
  • Warm paper, one serif for the editorial moments, mono for every number, and coral reserved for the single primary action. That is the rule the design system in sample 02 enforces.

Ties to the home page

Step 03, Make it visible, applied to the company's own argument. It is also the thesis in miniature: every claim traced to a source, and what cannot be traced left out.

02 / Design system

Step 05 · Learn and compound August 2026 Claude Design

One source of truth for the app, the deck and the site

What it is

A design system extracted from the product prototype and the sales deck, then promoted to the reference both must conform to.

Tokens, components in eight groups, a deck master with slide specimens, email templates, and written guidelines for color, type, spacing and voice. The handoff moved it to the company account as a project its tools can read.

The decision it served

Which surface is right when the app, the deck and the website disagree. The answer is written into the README: this system is correct, and the older prototype is stale.

Why it looks the way it does

  • Tokens are generated from the product repository's token files and never hand-edited, so the app and the system cannot drift. Change the source, recompile, and both move together.
  • The coral rule: coral marks exactly two things, the single primary action on a surface and failure. When everything is coral, nothing is.
  • The verdict palette: hue signals state in one place only. Sage passes, amber means a person must decide, coral fails. Severity is deliberately colorless, so "how bad" and "what happened" never compete.
  • Voice is part of the system. State the answer, then the evidence. Exact numbers, never rounded. Verbs on buttons name the consequence: Apply fix, Sign off, Skip.

Ties to the home page

Step 05, Learn and compound. The system took what the prototype had learned, gave it one shape, and became the thing every later prototype is checked against. Sample 03 shows that check being run.

03 / Product

Step 04 · Build July to August 2026 Claude Design

The remediation workspace, prototyped against the real engine

What it is

Working prototypes of the signed-in product: the project list, the document editor with its structure, order and contrast lenses, a guided review for people who are not tagging experts, a review inbox sorted by task, and an admin for the sales pipeline.

Built in Claude Design against a mirror of the product's own CSS, so the prototype and the app share classes, not just colors.

The decision it served

What a person should be asked to decide, and what the engine should settle on its own. The product is a workspace for real decisions, not a scanner that emits a report.

Why it looks the way it does

  • Dense on purpose: 13px body, 11 to 12px metadata, 40px rail buttons. The marketing layer is the opposite, and the system says never to average the two.
  • Every prototype operation was reconciled against the backend before handoff. The audit found six operations with no working path, named them, and kept the demo from implying a capability the engine did not have.
  • The admin prototype was audited against the design system afterwards: 34 hand-rolled classes, about 20 of which had a blessed primitive, and two invented hexes. All of it was rewritten onto the real classes, and the audit was kept as the record of what was wrong and why.
  • An empty record is a designed state. A new lead with no inventory gets one next action and a reason, because there is nothing to say in an email yet.
  • A prototype index names one canonical file per surface and marks everything else as spec, exploration or history, so nobody builds from the wrong artboard.

Ties to the home page

Step 04, Build, with the hero's fourth move as the working rule: check it against a source of truth. What the engine can prove is applied. What it cannot is flagged for a person, and the correction becomes signal.

04 / Decision record

Step 02 · Frame July 2026 Claude Code

Editor toolbar: docked, icon-first, three modes

What it is

A one-page ruling that closed the editor toolbar exploration after seven alternatives on the canvas: pods, trays, a command bar, tools in context.

The page carries the decided design as a working wireframe you can click, a table of what lives where, and the space math.

The decision it served

Where the editor's tools sit and how many modes there are. Ruled: docked in normal flow, never over the document; icons with tooltips and shortcuts; Preview removed as a mode, because it was only ever bounding-box visibility, so it became an eye toggle available in every mode.

Why it looks the way it does

  • Icon-first shrinks the bar's minimum width by roughly half. With the bar in normal flow instead of floating, the both-rails-open clipping bug becomes geometrically impossible rather than patched.
  • The two commit buttons keep their words. Tools are icons; the money buttons stay labeled, because an ambiguous Apply icon is a risk with no payoff.
  • Every icon carries an accessible name. In an accessibility product, icon-only cannot mean screen-reader-silent.

Ties to the home page

Decide from evidence, not opinion. The ruling states its reasoning, and the wireframe is live, so the room could try the decision before agreeing to it.

05 / Design review

Step 01 · Discover July 2026 Claude Code

One page, told two or three times

What it is

A review of the product's preliminary scan page, run on a real municipal crawl: the numbers as shown today beside the numbers proposed, seven stacked blocks beside three, the proposed page rendered with the real figures, and a ledger of every element with its action and reason.

The decision it served

What the page is for. It had become an analytics dashboard stapled to an inventory browser: the dashboard's three charts reappeared as the browser's filter controls, three document totals disagreed by section, and four page counts sat side by side unlabeled. Proposed: one headline number per concept, and one surface.

Why it looks the way it does

  • Seven figures with four meanings became four figures with one meaning each. The crawl-time estimate became a sublabel of the parsed count instead of a rival total.
  • A static chart was removed only where the same data already lived as an interactive filter. Nothing was cut for tidiness alone.
  • The ledger splits ownership between client layout and the API, so the counts and the CSV export agree at the source rather than being patched on screen.

Ties to the home page

The hero's first two moves. Information arrives messy; first, give it one shape. Then weight it by what matters, which is why the department table was demoted from headline to disclosure.

06 / Brand

Step 03 · Make it visible October 2026 Claude Design

A banner judged inside LinkedIn's frame

What it is

Two options for the Foresera company banner, a grade ladder and a before-and-after ring, composed on one canvas with a third artboard that shows how LinkedIn actually crops the banner and overlaps the logo on it.

The decision it served

Which option survives the crop. The company page covers the lower left of the banner with the logo, so the headline sits right of center and the proof sits far right, and both options were judged there rather than in isolation.

Why it looks the way it does

  • "Compliance is a checkbox. Usability is a person. We prove both." is the whole positioning in three lines, set in the same serif as the website.
  • The accent is a single color property on the artboard, so the two options were compared with everything else held constant.

Ties to the home page

Make it visible, in context. A banner approved on its own is a guess. One approved inside the frame it will live in is a decision.

07 / This site

Step 04 · Build October 2026 Claude Design to Claude Code

matthewbrown.design, from handoff to production

What it is

The page you came from. Designed in Claude Design as a working prototype with every animation timeline in readable source, handed off with a README, design tokens, content rules and a Claude Code prompt, then ported to a static site with one server route.

The decision it served

What to port verbatim and where to depart. The motion math, copy and tokens were ported exactly. The departures were made for accessibility and are recorded in the repository: hidden chapters hide with opacity only, so a screen reader still gets every chapter in reading order; case rails stay visible while a button has keyboard focus; the fit-to-viewport rule was extended to the method steps; and the hero is a direct canvas port rather than a video player.

Why it looks the way it does

  • The content rules are enforced by the build. It fails if an em dash reaches a built page.
  • The fit tool calls a server route that holds the key, and the build keeps it out of the public bundle.
  • After the port, three small changes came from looking at the live page: navy primary buttons, coral for everything that moves, and the static highlight color as one variable so two versions could be compared.

Ties to the home page

The method applied to itself. A thesis, a prototype the room could argue with, a build that kept the evidence, and a page that still changes when a correction comes in.
