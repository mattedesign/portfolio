01 / The thesis

Information arrives messy.

Interviews, documents, tickets, spreadsheets and policy, each in its own shape and none of it comparable.

First, give it one shape.

Everything is normalized into one schema, so a finding from a workshop and a finding from a file can sit side by side.

Then weight it by what matters.

Not every finding is equal. Each one is weighted by its impact on the decision being made, so the important things rise.

Check it against a source of truth.

Every claim is traced to evidence. What holds up is confirmed. What does not is flagged for a person to review.

Every correction teaches the system.

When a person fixes one flagged item, the fix becomes signal and resolves the same pattern everywhere it appears.

More information makes it better, not slower.

That is the whole idea. Strategy and building are one practice, and the system keeps getting smarter with use.

02 / How I work

One practice, from the first question to the system that keeps answering it.

Five steps. Each one produces something people can see, argue with and act on. The last step feeds the first.

  1. Step 01

    Discover

    Interviews and workshops with the people who own the decision, and the people who live with it.

    • Stakeholder interviews
    • Executive workshops
    • Evidence inventory
  2. Step 02

    Frame

    Synthesize what we heard into a problem worth solving and the opportunities around it.

    • Problem statement
    • Opportunity areas
    • Decision criteria
  3. Step 03

    Make it visible

    Put the current and future state where everyone can see it, so the room argues about the same thing.

    • Journey maps
    • Service blueprints
    • Experience vision
    • Roadmap
  4. Step 04

    Build

    Align design intent with technology and delivery teams, then ship the thinnest version that proves it.

    • Service concept
    • Architecture brief
    • Working system
  5. Step 05

    Learn and compound

    Validate with evidence. Every correction and outcome feeds back, so the system and the strategy improve with use.

    • Validation evidence
    • Human review loop
    • The next question
Then it loops

Back to Discover, with better evidence than the last pass.

Case 01

  • Case 01
  • Public sector
  • Document accessibility
  • Strategy through build

A city asked for compliance. The real question was exposure.

A proof of concept on a major U.S. city's public document library, run end to end: discovery, an executive readout and a working system now live in production with human review.

01 The problem as first stated

"Our public documents need to pass an accessibility checker before the deadline."

A reasonable ask. Most of the market answers it by auto-tagging files until the checker turns green.

02 The real problem, after discovery

Passing a checker is not the same as being usable.

Leadership was not asking about tags. They were asking about legal exposure.

The decision the city actually faced was which documents put it at risk, and in what order to fix them.

03 Who was in the room

Five groups. One decision.

  • City leadership
  • Legal counsel
  • Accessibility and compliance
  • IT and records
  • Department content owners

I ran discovery and the live proof of concept, then structured the readout for the people who had to sign off.

04 The artifact that aligned them

A five-question decision path for the executive readout.

  1. 1 Liability Where are we exposed today?
  2. 2 Feasibility Can it be fixed in the files we already have?
  3. 3 Capability What does it take to keep it fixed?
  4. 4 Usability Can people using assistive technology use it?
  5. 5 Scale Does it hold across the whole library?

Underneath the path

Thousands of technical violations become one grade a city manager can act on.

  • A
  • B
  • C
  • D
  • F

A risk-scoring model weights every finding by disability impact and public exposure, so the riskiest documents surface first.

05 What got built

A working system, live in production with human review.

  • A remediation engine Repairs reading order, tables, alt text and forms in Word, PowerPoint and PDF, then verifies against PDF/UA.
  • A defensible record Fingerprinting removes duplicates first and keeps a tamper-evident chain of custody.
  • Proof of no harm A regression suite and visual-fidelity gate prove nothing was damaged.
  • Room to grow New document types, rules and domains plug in without a rebuild.

06 What got smarter over time

One correction fixes the pattern, not just the file.

Every reviewer correction becomes signal. The same approach became the foundation of a company now selling to state, local and higher-education buyers.
